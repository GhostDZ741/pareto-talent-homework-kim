2. Three competitor lead magnets, up close (mystery-shopped)

Athena · "How well do you actually delegate?"

  • Format: a 10-question quiz.
  • Promise: "Get a clear picture of where the gaps are, what's holding you back, and what it takes to delegate more effectively."
  • Who it's for: leaders who delegate.
  • Capture and qualification: first name, last name and email after the last question. The score and level go into hidden fields. No revenue question.
  • After opt-in (observed Oct 4): a redirect to a thank-you page: "Your result is on its way. Check your inbox." The result is emailed, not shown. The page sells the EA ("Your result tells you where you are. An Athena EA helps you get to the next level.") with a Ryan Hoover testimonial and the CTA "Find your EA".
  • Gap: you learn your level, not what to do on Monday.

BELAY · "The EA Advantage" guide

  • Format: a gated PDF guide.
  • Promise: how an EA "helps you grow through every stage of business".
  • Who it's for: founders at any stage.
  • Capture and qualification: first name, last name and email required; phone optional, with an SMS consent box. No qualification on the guide itself. Revenue is only asked on the separate "Book your call" form.
  • After opt-in (observed): an inline message on the same page, "This resource is on it's way to your inbox! Want to take the next step…", plus a "Schedule a Call" button and a 6-question "delegation bottleneck" quiz.
  • Gap: generic content, the same for every reader.

South · "10X Delegation Course: 50+ Tasks to Delegate"

  • Format: a free email course (Beehiiv newsletter).
  • Promise: "Free checklist of 50+ tasks to delegate to make money in your sleep".
  • Who it's for: growing owners and managers.
  • Capture and qualification: email only. No qualification.
  • After opt-in (observed): a checkmark on the button, no thank-you page, no redirect. Delivery happens by email course.
  • Gap: a list of tasks, with no order and no instructions for handing them off.

Patterns 1. Every competitor delivers by email and uses the moment after opt-in to push a call. 2. None qualifies by revenue at the lead-magnet step. 3. None gives the founder a personal plan. They give a level, a list or a guide.

3. What already exists, so we don't duplicate it

  • Pareto's own assets: the free HIRE book (how to find people you don't have to manage), an ROI example on the homepage, the "Bottleneck Architect" AI prompt inside a guide, 4 guides, and a $47 live training.
  • Formats that are everywhere: delegation PDFs, "tasks you can delegate" lists, salary and savings calculators, quizzes.
  • The gap: a tool where the founder ticks their own tasks and gets back hours, cost and a step-by-step handoff plan with the briefs written. Nobody has it, Pareto included.
  • Our answer: The 40-Hour Handoff Plan.

4. Ad landscape (what's running, Oct 4)

  • BELAY runs the deepest machine: vertical guides, client stories, and a rematch guarantee. Best hook: "Your best closer is stuck doing admin."
  • Somewhere: speed plus risk reversal, "5 pre-vetted candidates in 5 days. You only pay if you hire." It runs a retargeting sequence with creator Nick Huber.
  • Virtual Latinos: price and time, "Still working weekends?", "Starting at $1.6K/month".
  • Pareto: a 25-ad campaign for the free HIRE book (Oct 2–3). Best hook: "Why does every assistant I hire end up needing me more?"
  • Nobody runs on Meta: AI-trained talent as the message, a speed promise ("3 profiles in 24 hours"), or a tool that writes the founder's own plan.

Top objections, and Pareto's honest answers

Objection Honest counter
"It's faster to do it myself." Freedom 40: 40 hours back in 30 days or next month free (conditions: Mastermind, onboarding plan, 10 minutes a day).
"I've been burned before." Lifetime Replacement + 3+ candidates you interview + 1,000+ applicants per placement.
"It's too expensive." Founder-time math. But Athena costs the same, so the win must be selection, not price.
"I don't know what to delegate." The lead magnet itself.

The insight the lead magnet is built on:

"The first hire usually fails not because they are bad, but because you handed off the task without handing off the context" (Indie Hackers)

6. ICP and personas

ICP: Founders of service businesses (agencies, law firms, consultancies, coaching companies) doing $100K+ a month, who are personally the bottleneck: every client, decision and approval still goes through them. The founder decides and the founder pays.

Personas, by cause, in their words

Persona The trigger In their words
The Ceiling Everything routes through them "growth stalls at exactly the ceiling of what one person can personally touch"
The Burned A VA failed and they blame themselves "I keep thinking it was me until other staff and a second company brought time etc on and had the same experiences."
The Stacked Staff Managing people became the job "I'm still creating more work for me, not less work for me"
The Overpaying Paying a premium EA brand, still checking every task "This is not high-leverage support. It is task execution that still requires close oversight"
The Reluctant Has the budget, not the picture "The first month of hiring my Belay assistant, I didn't know what tasks to give them. It was awkward."
